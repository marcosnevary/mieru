# mieru

見える, "to be visible": learn Japanese vocabulary by linking each word directly to an image instead of to its English translation.

## Who it is for

Anki users learning Japanese who want picture cards without assembling furigana, audio and images by hand.

## What it does

- Type an English word; choose among the Japanese words the dictionary offers, each with its reading and meanings.
- Each word becomes three cards: comprehension (Japanese word → picture, with the reading revealed on the back), production (picture → Japanese word) and writing (picture and pronunciation → draw the word by hand on the card, then compare it with the stroke order, shown behind the drawing, and keep drawing to correct it). Each card plays a native recording (or synthesized speech when none exists): on the answer for comprehension and production, on the question for writing.
- Choose one or more pictures that represent the word; they carry the meaning, with no English on the cards. Pictures come from the search or from image files on disk. When the word itself makes a poor picture search (nice to meet you), search pictures for a more concrete term (handshake) without losing the chosen Japanese word.
- Write a personal connection to the word (a memory, a place, a person); it is required, and it shows on the back of every card.
- Type a list of words (dog, cat, bank) and go through them one by one, choosing the Japanese word and picture for each; every word is loaded up front, and you can move back and forth or skip any you don't want.

## Out of scope for now

- Japanese → picture cards for words typed in Japanese.
- Example sentences, pitch accent, kanji breakdowns.
- Picture sources other than Pixabay.
